# Tokyo, Japan

## Overview
Tokyo works best when planned by neighborhood rather than by trying to cross the city repeatedly for individual landmarks. A strong first trip mixes traditional districts, big modern hubs, food stops, skyline views, and one or two slower local neighborhoods. Four full days is the minimum for a first visit, while five to seven days gives enough room for better pacing and a day trip.

## Neighborhoods
- **Shibuya**: scramble crossing, shopping, nightlife, and an easy first-time base.
- **Harajuku and Omotesando**: youth culture, crepes, Meiji Shrine access, and designer-lined avenues.
- **Shinjuku**: neon city energy, gardens, nightlife alleys, shopping, and major rail connections.
- **Asakusa**: old Tokyo atmosphere centered on Senso-ji and classic snack streets.
- **Ueno**: museums, park space, and access to Ameyoko market.
- **Yanaka**: a lower-rise, slower, more old-fashioned side of Tokyo.
- **Ginza and Tsukiji**: polished shopping plus seafood breakfast and market browsing.
- **Kuramae, Nakameguro, and Shimokitazawa**: cafes, design shops, vintage, ceramics, and a more local feel.

## Must-do sights
- **Meiji Shrine and Yoyogi Park** for an easy shrine-plus-city contrast.
- **Shibuya Crossing** and one skyline deck such as Shibuya Sky.
- **Shinjuku Gyoen** for a slower green break in the middle of the city.
- **Senso-ji and Nakamise** for classic temple-street Tokyo.
- **Tsukiji Outer Market** for seafood, snacks, and food-focused wandering.
- **Ueno Park** and the **Tokyo National Museum** if museums are a priority.
- **Tokyo Skytree** or another observation deck for big city views.
- **teamLab** for a modern art contrast to the older neighborhoods.

## Food notes
- Tokyo is one of the easiest cities to eat well across all budgets.
- Core first-time foods include sushi, ramen, yakitori, tonkatsu, izakaya meals, onigiri, tempura, and dessert stops in Harajuku or neighborhood cafes.
- Good food-focused areas include Tsukiji, Asakusa, Shibuya, Shinjuku, and Yurakucho.
- Specialty add-ons worth considering include kitchenware shopping in Kappabashi, monjayaki in Tsukishima, and slower cafe hopping in Kuramae or Nakameguro.

## Getting around
- **IC cards** (Suica or Pasmo — functionally identical) are the standard way to pay for trains, buses, and even convenience store purchases. iPhone users can add a virtual IC card via the Wallet app; Android support is limited to phones purchased in Japan.
- **Google Maps** works well for Tokyo public transit routing, including fare estimates.
- **Trains stop running around midnight** — plan to be at the station by 11:30 PM at the latest if changing lines. Taxis are expensive as a late-night fallback.
- A **JR Pass is not needed** if staying within Tokyo only; it becomes worthwhile if also traveling to other cities (e.g. Kyoto, Osaka) by shinkansen.
- Basic train etiquette: move fully into the train car rather than blocking doors, keep backpacks in front of you when crowded, and avoid talking loudly or letting headphone audio leak.

## Where to stay
First-time visitors are generally best based near **Shibuya, Shinjuku, or Asakusa**. Look for accommodation within a 10-minute walk of a station so you lose less time on every day of the trip.
- **Budget**: hostels/poshtels in Asakusa; business hotels near Shimbashi station.
- **Mid-range**: hotels in Ginza (convenient, central, near flagship shopping) or Shinagawa (close to both Shibuya and Haneda Airport).
- **Luxury**: hotels in Roppongi (elevated city views) or similar high-end districts.

## Suggested structure
### 3 days
- **Day 1**: Harajuku, Meiji Shrine, Yoyogi Park, and Shibuya.
- **Day 2**: Shinjuku, Shinjuku Gyoen, a viewpoint, and evening alleys or bars.
- **Day 3**: Asakusa, Senso-ji, Ueno, and optionally Yanaka.

### 4 to 5 days
- Add **Tsukiji**, **teamLab**, **Ginza**, or a slower neighborhood day in **Kuramae**, **Nakameguro**, or **Shimokitazawa**.
- Use the extra time to avoid overstuffing each day and to revisit neighborhoods after dark.

### 6 to 7 days
- Add one day trip such as **Hakone**, **Kamakura**, **Nikko**, or the **Fuji Five Lakes**.
- Keep one flexible day for shopping, museums, anime or electronics districts, or any attractions that need advance booking.

## Practical tips
- Start famous sights early. This matters especially for Senso-ji, Tsukiji, skyline decks, and popular modern attractions.
- Book ahead for teamLab, Shibuya Sky, Ghibli Museum, Disney, sumo, or any other timed-entry experience.
- Carry some cash. Tokyo is more card-friendly than many parts of Japan, but some smaller places still prefer cash.
- Public rubbish bins are limited, so carry a small bag for wrappers or bottles.
- Google Translate's camera-based translation is useful for Japanese-only menus.
- Visit Japan Web (Japan's online immigration/customs service) should be completed before arrival.
- Tokyo is generally considered very safe, including for solo travelers, with the notable exception of bar-related scams in Kabukicho at night.
- Comfortable walking shoes are important — Tokyo is very walkable and taxis are costly.
